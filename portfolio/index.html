Bible Arcade

A free arcade of Bible games for churches, youth groups and families.

The problem

Youth leaders want group games that feel as fun as the apps kids already love. Most Bible games feel like homework, and the popular quiz tools all feel the same.

Who it's for

Youth leaders running a room, and the kids, teens and families who keep playing at home.

Key decisions
  1. Pivoted early. My first version was a single quiz game. Testing it, I saw it felt like every other quiz tool, so I widened it into an arcade of 8 different games.
  2. Removed friction. Anyone can play instantly as a guest. Signing in is optional and only needed to save progress and join leaderboards.
  3. Designed for a young audience. A name filter, player reports and automatic cleanup of inactive accounts were built in from day one.
  4. Built the growth loop in. One leader brings a whole room. Every game ends with "Challenge a friend", and shared links show a rich preview.
  5. Spent $0 until traction. Free hosting with offline caching. I chose not to pay for an upgrade until real usage justifies it.
Outcome
  • 3 daysidea to live product
  • 8games at launch
  • $0monthly running cost
What I'd measure next
  • Rooms hosted per week, the north-star metric for groups
  • Share rate of "Challenge a friend" links
  • Week-1 return rate of new players
Working prototype · research

Kin

An app that helps people keep in touch with the people who matter.

The problem

People lose touch without noticing. Existing "personal CRM" apps make it worse: when I used one, it showed 103 people as "overdue", and that guilt made me avoid the app entirely.

The insight

Never show a backlog. Show one person at a time, and always suggest what to say, so reaching out takes less effort than not.

Key decisions
  1. Privacy and cost over hype. I didn't send personal messages to a paid AI service. I built my own math-based engine, so it costs almost nothing per user.
  2. Let the data change the plan. Tested on 16,000 real messages, "will they text tomorrow?" turned out to be barely predictable. So I re-focused the product on spotting long silences and drift, where it works.
  3. Tested on real data, not just a demo. My practice benchmark looked great, but real chats exposed mistakes. I fixed the model rather than the test.
Outcome
  • 61% → 85%accuracy on everyday sentences
  • 60×faster after fixes
  • 1research paper written
What I'd measure next
  • % of nudges that lead to a message within 48 hours
  • Friendships "re-warmed" per user per month
  • Whether users still open the app after week 4
Personal AI assistant · operations

Hermes: questioning the dashboard

An AI assistant I run that reads my email, fills my calendar and runs daily jobs.

The problem

It got slower and more expensive, but the usage dashboard said everything was normal.

What I found

The dashboard only counted conversations that started that month. One conversation open since July was re-reading its whole history on every message, and it was invisible.

Outcome
  • 5×real usage vs. reported
  • ~50%wasted steps cut from a daily job
  • 1 fixstopped the leak
The lesson

Don't trust a metric until you know exactly what it counts. I now check how a number is defined before I make decisions with it.
